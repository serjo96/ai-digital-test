# Shelf Ready — implementation note

**Status.** Stages 1–5 and the minimal stage 6 architecture stabilization are complete. Accepted B1-v2 accounts for all 220 rows as 156 canonical products over 216 product rows plus four non-products. B3 generation and independent claim verification ran on the full input and reproduce offline. All 156 products have a draft in the current runtime/UI; the two identity-uncertain previews remain unpublished and in review. The reviewer completed the compact 20-item matching audit: 20/20 reviewed, 18/18 agreement, 18/20 scored coverage, 2 `unknown`, 0 disagreements. The export and metrics are saved in `eval/matching-audit-human-verified.json` and `eval/matching-audit-metrics.json` as post-holdout validation.

**Architecture.** A Node 24 / TypeScript Nest standalone CLI reads the original feed and closed taxonomy, keeps offers separate from canonical products, preserves exact source evidence, and stores immutable run reports and metrics. A thin dispatcher delegates B0/B1, B2, B3, and comparison to separate run services; filesystem operations and AI provider registration have dedicated providers, while domain logic remains pure functions. B3 generates only from allowed supports and fail-closed verifies complete atomic claims. React reads a validated snapshot and does not call models or recompute decisions.

**Three trade-offs.** (1) Conservative matching preserves variants and accessories; uncertain relations stay separate or in review. (2) B2 remains rejected: the historical local Ollama experiments failed the quality gate, and a later OpenAI development-only extraction check produced 11 correct, 3 unexpected, and 0 missing semantic additions but still failed the existing acceptance criteria. It did not alter B1 decisions or exercise shadow matching. The accepted product baseline therefore remains deterministic B1, while the separate OpenAI B3 stage handles only publication wording and claim verification. (3) Saved JSON/cache and an offline viewer make the result reproducible, at the cost of a deliberate manual signoff rather than an automatic approval workflow.

**Evidence and failures.** Full-input B3 produced 154/156 ready products, two identity-review products, no technically withheld products, and 390 structurally valid published claims. The two review items receive short previews assembled only from allowed identity evidence; they are not model-verified and `publishedText` stays null. The live run used 322 calls, 526449 tokens and $8.110955; one malformed verifier support ID was rejected and recovered by the single allowed repair. Offline replay reproduced both historical hashes with zero calls. The earlier family-level holdout metrics remain provisional; the compact human audit was selected after holdout disclosure and is therefore post-holdout validation, not a fresh independent holdout. Two human `unknown` answers are excluded rather than counted as successes.

**Deliberate cuts.** This take-home does not add HTTP controllers, a database/queue, a full ports-and-adapters layer, or a separate browser contracts package. Web keeps its existing backend imports, and historical reports/experiments remain untouched. Those boundaries can be introduced if a second transport, storage implementation, or deployable frontend becomes real; doing so now would add migration risk without improving the assignment criteria. No additional model run or review of all 108 provisional family-label rows is required.
